West Hollywood City Council · November 3, 2026

West Hollywood Council Race: Development and Campaign Money

West Hollywood–Fairfax Indivisible · Updated October 21, 2026 with the October 19 Fountain Avenue vote. Every fact is linked to its source.

At a glance · The zoning and housing fights · The Fountain Avenue vote · Follow the money · Where the candidates stand · Sources and what is not yet confirmed

At a glance

Three West Hollywood City Council seats are on the November 3 ballot, and 14 candidates are running (WEHO Times, Aug 14, 2026). Chelsea Byers is the only sitting councilmember running. Mayor John Heilman and Councilmember Lauren Meister are term-limited. Councilmember John Erickson's seat is not up this year; he is running for State Senate District 24 (Beverly Press, Jul 15, 2026).

Housing growth has split the current council. On April 6, 2026, Byers, Erickson and Vice Mayor Danny Hang voted down Heilman's request for a staff discussion of the state upzoning law SB 79; Heilman and Meister voted for it (WEHOonline, May 14, 2026). Two of the three open seats belong to that two-vote minority. The same split decided the October 19 vote against studying a Fountain Avenue design without protected bike lanes (see The Fountain Avenue vote).

If Erickson wins the Senate race, his council seat opens too, and the council must appoint a replacement or call a special election (WEHOonline, Sep 29, 2026).

The zoning and housing fights

The fights are over zoning and who approves projects, not the building code. The council adopted the 2025 state building codes in February 2026 with no reported controversy (agenda item, Feb 2, 2026).

Issue What it is Status, October 2026
SB 79 and a local alternative plan The state law, in effect since July 1, 2026, allows taller housing near transit, including West Hollywood's three planned K Line stations. Residents asked the city to file a local alternative plan (a TODAP) that places the same housing elsewhere. No plan filed as of July 12; staff were told on June 15 to return with options. City staff said more than 1,100 rent-stabilized units could be affected without added protections. (WEHOonline, Jul 12; minutes, Jun 15; WEHOonline, Jun 27)
Faster approvals ("streamlining") Qualifying housing projects would be approved by the Community Development Director instead of at a Planning Commission hearing; larger projects would still go to the commission. Appeals would go straight to the council. Tabled June 29 pending the state ethics question below; the city lists it as "continued to a date uncertain." (Engage WeHo)
Byers's job and the FPPC question Byers is Municipal Policy Campaigns Director at Welcoming Neighbors Network, a national pro-housing advocacy group. The City Attorney asked the state Fair Political Practices Commission whether that bars her from housing votes. Byers says her employer has "no financial membership structure or relationships with any for-profit entities who would benefit from these decisions." No answer as of September 17. This is an open question, not a finding of wrongdoing. (WEHOonline, Jul 15; Beverly Press, Jul 1; WEHOonline, Sep 17)
Rezoning study (ZIP) The city's own rezoning study to meet a state target of 3,933 homes by 2029 (WEHOonline, May 6, 2025). Residents' groups in single-family areas have organized against upzoning (WEHOonline, Aug 2, 2025). Advisory only so far; binding hearings expected in summer 2027. (Engage WeHo)
Projects that bypass local height limits State builder's remedy and density bonus laws let some projects exceed local limits, such as six stories on a street zoned for two at 833 Westbourne. 833 Westbourne approved May 7; four more builder's remedy projects reported pending. An appeal of a six-story project on Shoreham Drive awaits a council hearing. (WEHOonline, May 8; WEHOonline, Jun 17)
Lost rent-stabilized housing 120 rent-stabilized units were withdrawn under the Ellis Act after the city resumed taking applications in fall 2023. Asked how many demolished properties had been rebuilt, the city's rent manager said "None." A review of the rent stabilization ordinance is under way; the demolition review is expected in spring 2027. (WEHOonline, Apr 25; WEHOonline, Sep 1)
Billboards traded for development Developers offer housing or public benefits in exchange for billboards off the Sunset Strip. 1000 N. La Brea (CIM Group) would be 34 stories with six billboards under a deal that could run 55 years. Planning Commission approved 1000 N. La Brea on October 1; the council decides next. (WEHOonline, Oct 5)

The Fountain Avenue vote

On October 19, 2026, the council voted 3-2 against Lauren Meister's request that staff study a Fountain Avenue design that keeps street parking and leaves out the protected bike lanes. Byers, Erickson and Vice Mayor Danny Hang voted no; Mayor John Heilman and Meister voted yes. The approved design stays on track, and the city says construction of the first phase will begin before the end of the year (LAist, Oct 20; WEHOonline news analysis, Oct 20).

This is the same 3-2 split as on SB 79. Meister and Heilman, the two yes votes, are term-limited, and both have endorsed Kyle Brazeal, who opposes the bike lanes (LAist, Oct 19).

Background

  • Traffic and safety. Fountain carries about 35,000 vehicles a day and has 120 driveways along the project stretch (WEHOonline, Mar 22). City figures show 35 severe-injury collisions and five fatal collisions on Fountain from 2013 to 2024. Blake Ackerman, a 27-year-old attorney, was killed in a hit-and-run while biking on Fountain in 2025 (LAist, Oct 19).
  • The grant. The staff report puts $4.609 million of a state Sustainable Transportation Equity Project grant toward construction. Staff said dropping the protected bike lanes would depart from the approved scope and could put that money at risk unless the California Air Resources Board agreed to the change. Streets For All said the city would have to return the money; the staff report did not say so (WEHOonline, Oct 15).
  • The second phase. Wider sidewalks, ADA curb ramps, trees, new signals and utility relocation come later, at a preliminary estimate of $40 million to $50 million over several years (WEHOonline, Oct 15).
  • The lawsuit. Michael J. Kilroy, who owns an eight-unit building at Flores and Fountain, sued the city on October 29, 2025. He argues the council wrongly exempted the first phase from state environmental review (CEQA) and did not study traffic pushed onto side streets. As of May, no injunction had been issued and a hearing was set for August 3 (Los Angeles Superior Court, No. 25STCP04203; WEHOonline, May 7). At the October 19 meeting, Heilman cited the case as pending (WEHOonline, Oct 20).
  • What comes next. The construction contract still has to come back to the council for approval (WEHOonline, Aug 29). Final construction documents were expected by the end of October (WEHOonline, Oct 15).

What was said on October 19

  • Meister said her alternative "might accomplish the same safety objectives while preserving parking" (LAist), and: "If listening to residents is my legacy, then so be it."
  • Byers: "I'm simply not interested in moving forward with a project that eliminates the protected bike lane."
  • Erickson called Meister's item an "11th hour election coup."
  • Heilman: "This is not a debate about parking versus safety." He said the lawsuit was a reason to have a backup design.
  • Streets For All, which endorses Byers, Krieger and Post, mobilized bike lane supporters before the meeting (LAist).

Quotes other than Meister's first are from WEHOonline's news analysis, Oct 20.

Follow the money

The biggest checks go to outside committees, which candidates do not control. Gifts to a candidate are capped at $1,000 per person (city code §2.76.030), but outside committees have taken single gifts of up to $75,000. The committee backing Byers, Krieger and Cottrell got its largest gift, $20,000, from Orange Barrel Media, one of four early donors WEHOonline calls "billboard-linked." The committee backing Brazeal got $125,000 from a franchise-business PAC, the state hotel PAC and a landlord-and-Realtor PAC.
