Senate leadership vote scheduled for December 1

Why we support Chris Van Hollen for Senate leader

Senate Democrats will choose their leader by secret ballot in a vote that is scheduled for December 1 and may be moved earlier. Our chapter is asking Senators Padilla and Schiff to vote for Chris Van Hollen of Maryland over Chuck Schumer, for the reasons below.

Van Hollen and Kilmar Abrego Garcia

In March 2025 the Trump administration deported Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Maryland resident, to a prison in El Salvador despite a court order barring his removal there, and later acknowledged the error. Van Hollen flew to El Salvador the following month. He was refused a visit and a phone call, and soldiers stopped his car near the prison, before officials brought Abrego Garcia to meet him at his hotel. Van Hollen said he had gone to defend the right to due process, which protects everyone.

Sources: NPR, ABC News, 7News.

Republican spending bill, March 2025
SchumerVoted to advance itSaid on March 12 that Republicans did not have the votes to advance the bill, then announced the next day that he would vote to advance it. Nine members of his caucus voted with him, and the motion cleared the 60-vote threshold.
Van HollenVoted noVoted against advancing the bill, with most of the caucus.
Sources: Roll Call, The Fiscal Times
Arms sales to Israel
SchumerVoted to let them proceedOpposed resolutions to block sales in July 2025 and April 2026. In April 2026 he was one of seven Democrats to do so, against 40 who voted to block the sale of bulldozers.
Van HollenVoted to block themSupported the 2025 and 2026 resolutions, co-sponsored the 2026 measure on bombs, and has backed these measures since the first Senate votes in November 2024.
Sources: Associated Press, The Jewish Chronicle, Sen. Sanders’ office
Funding for the war in Iran
SchumerHas not committedVoted for a war powers resolution to end the war, but had not said as of November 13 whether he would block the December funding bill over it.
Van HollenWould cut it offSays he will not support a government funding bill unless it ends funding for the war, even if that leads to a shutdown.
Sources: The Intercept, Punchbowl News, NBC News
ICE
SchumerFund it with reformsWas noncommittal in early January 2026. After the killings of Renee Good and Alex Pretti in Minneapolis, he said he would oppose ICE funding until the agency was overhauled, and Democrats blocked the Homeland Security bill.
Van HollenTake back its fundingSaid in January 2026 that he would not vote for one more dime for ICE operations under Trump. He now says Congress should take back the money Republicans set aside for ICE and use the budget to restrict the agency.
Sources: Semafor, Truthout, Senate Democrats, The American Prospect
Medicare for All
SchumerNot a cosponsorIs not among the cosponsors of the Medicare for All Act. He has focused on affordability and on the Affordable Care Act.
Van HollenCosponsorSigned on to the Medicare for All Act in November 2025 and has said Congress should extend the Affordable Care Act tax credits and then move to Medicare for All.
Sources: Congress.gov, Common Dreams, Axios
AIPAC money
SchumerAccepts itFEC filings show about $95,000 from AIPAC’s PAC, including donations it forwarded from its donors.
Van HollenTakes noneFEC filings show nothing from AIPAC’s PAC or its super PAC, and he has said no one should accept AIPAC money. He has received about $134,000 from other pro-Israel PACs, chiefly J Street, which has opposed AIPAC on issues such as the Iran agreement.
Sources: Who Funds My Rep: Schumer, Van Hollen
Schumer’s record as leader
  • Schumer first backed other candidates in this year’s Michigan and Maine primaries. Abdul El-Sayed and Troy Jackson won those primaries and then won their seats. (PolitiFact, Time)
  • In the Michigan primary, AIPAC’s super PAC spent about $30 million against El-Sayed and for his opponent, the most it has spent on a single race. (Newsweek)
  • In an NBC News poll taken in late October, 13 percent of registered voters viewed Schumer positively. (The Hill)
  • At least six incoming Democratic senators have said they will not vote for him. (The Hill)
Van Hollen’s record and commitments
  • He chaired the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee from 2017 to 2019, after leading House Democrats’ campaign committee. (Wikipedia, Yahoo News)
  • He says the Senate leader should stay out of open primaries and that the party’s Senate campaign committee should refuse AIPAC money. (Time)
  • He wants to abolish the filibuster and to end super PACs. (The Hill, The Washington Sun)
  • He has ruled out running for president in 2028. (The Hill)
  • He supported the 2015 Iran nuclear agreement, which Schumer opposed. (Baltimore Sun, PBS NewsHour)
  • Senators Bernie Sanders and Jeff Merkley and Senator-elect Peggy Flanagan have endorsed him. (CNN, The Hill)
