Senate leadership vote, December 1

Schumer and Van Hollen: the records, side by side

Senate Democrats will choose between Chuck Schumer of New York, their leader since 2017, and Chris Van Hollen of Maryland. Our chapter is asking Senators Padilla and Schiff to vote for Van Hollen. This page lays out the record behind that request, with a source for each item.

The short version

  • When it counted, Schumer gave up leverage. In March 2025 he said Democrats would block the Republican spending bill, then reversed himself the next day and voted to advance it. Van Hollen voted no.
  • Schumer has voted against most of his own caucus on arms sales to Israel. Van Hollen has voted to block those sales since the first resolutions were offered.
  • The money is different. Schumer accepts AIPAC-linked and corporate PAC money, and Wall Street has been his largest source of funds. Van Hollen has taken nothing from AIPAC.
  • Schumer worked against candidates who went on to win. He backed opponents of two incoming Democratic senators in their primaries.
  • Voters have lost confidence in him. In an NBC News poll taken just before the election, 13 percent of registered voters viewed his leadership positively.
Call Senators Padilla and Schiff

Key votes

March 2025: advancing the Republican spending bill

SchumerVoted yesSaid Democrats would block the bill, then announced the next day that he would vote to advance it. Nine members of his caucus followed him, which gave Republicans the 60 votes they needed.
Van HollenVoted noVoted against advancing the bill, with most of the caucus.
Sources: Roll Call, The Fiscal Times

July 2025: blocking sales of bombs and rifles to Israel

SchumerVoted no, twiceOpposed both resolutions even though more than half of Senate Democrats supported the one on rifles. He voted no again on a later arms-sale resolution during the Iran war.
Van HollenVoted yesSupported both resolutions, and has backed these measures since the first ones were offered early in the Gaza war.
Sources: Associated Press, The Nation, The Intercept

2015: the Iran nuclear agreement

SchumerOpposed itBroke with President Obama and was the first Senate Democrat to announce opposition to the agreement.
Van HollenSupported itBacked the agreement as a House member and defended it during his 2016 Senate campaign.
Sources: PBS NewsHour, Baltimore Sun

Now: funding for the war in Iran

SchumerNo stated planHas criticized the administration’s handling of the war but has not said whether he would withhold government funding to end it.
Van HollenWould withhold fundingSays Democrats should refuse to pass a funding bill that does not end the war, even if that leads to a shutdown.
Sources: Punchbowl News, NBC News

Who funds them

Campaign-finance trackers count this money in different ways, so totals vary. We give the narrowest and broadest published figures and link to each.

AIPAC and other pro-Israel PACs

SchumerAccepts itAbout $95,000 from AIPAC’s PAC and super PAC and about $516,000 from pro-Israel PACs in total, by one count of FEC filings. OpenSecrets’ broader count, which includes individual donors, is about $1.7 million over his career.
Van HollenNo AIPAC moneyFEC filings show nothing from AIPAC’s PAC or super PAC, and he has said no one should accept it. He has received about $134,000 from other pro-Israel PACs, chiefly J Street, which often opposes AIPAC.
Sources: Who Funds My Rep: Schumer, Van Hollen, Common Dreams, citing OpenSecrets

Wall Street

SchumerHis largest sourceReceived nearly $9 million from the securities and investment industry between 1989 and 2010. It was still his top contributing industry from 2013 to 2018.
Van HollenNot a major sourceWe found no comparable reporting on Van Hollen and the financial industry.
Sources: CBS News, Albany Times Union

Corporate PACs

SchumerAccepts themFEC records show contributions from corporate and trade-association PACs, including financial firms.
Van HollenMixed recordAccepted corporate PAC money as a House member and has since said he refuses it. FEC records still show a Bank of America PAC contribution in 2022, so this is not a clean record.
Sources: ProPublica: Schumer, ProPublica: Van Hollen (House), ProPublica: Van Hollen (Senate), Washington Free Beacon

Schumer’s record as leader

  • The March 2025 reversal. House Democrats had voted almost unanimously against the Republican spending bill. Schumer’s change of position a day after promising to block it divided the party and prompted calls for him to step aside. (Roll Call)
  • Taking sides in primaries. Schumer backed Rep. Haley Stevens over Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan and opposed Peggy Flanagan in Minnesota. Both won their primaries and their general elections. AIPAC’s super PAC spent more against El-Sayed than on any other race. (Time, The Hill, NOTUS)
  • Standing with voters. An NBC News poll in late October found that 13 percent of registered voters viewed Schumer’s leadership positively. A year earlier, a Puck and Echelon Insights poll found that fewer than half of Democratic voters viewed him favorably. (The Hill, Puck)
  • Standing with the new majority. At least six incoming Democratic senators have said they will not vote for him, and Senators Sanders and Slotkin have said the same. (The Hill)

Van Hollen’s record

  • Experience running a caucus campaign. He chaired the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee from 2017 to 2019 and served in House Democratic leadership before that. (Wikipedia, CNN)
  • What he says he would change. The leader would stay out of open-seat primaries, and the party’s Senate campaign committee would stop accepting AIPAC money. He names restoring Medicaid funding, extending Affordable Care Act tax credits, and restoring nutrition programs as first priorities. (Time, Fox News)

What Van Hollen did for Kilmar Abrego Garcia

In March 2025 the Trump administration deported Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Maryland resident, to a prison in El Salvador despite a 2019 court order that barred his removal to that country. The administration acknowledged the deportation was a mistake, and El Salvador’s president said he would not return him.

  • He went in person. Van Hollen flew to El Salvador on April 16, 2025, after promising the family he would do everything he could.
  • He pressed the government for answers. The vice president told him that El Salvador was holding Abrego Garcia because the Trump administration was paying it to, and offered no evidence of the gang ties the administration had alleged.
  • He kept going after being refused. He was denied a visit and a phone call, and soldiers stopped his car near the prison. The next day the government allowed a meeting at his hotel.
  • He was the first outside contact. No one had been able to reach Abrego Garcia since his deportation. Van Hollen learned he had been moved out of the CECOT prison and reported on his condition to his family.
  • He framed it as a constitutional question. He said the trip was about the right to due process, which protects everyone, and not about defending one man.

Sources: NPR, ABC News, 7News.

To be fair

Schumer’s supporters make a serious case, and Senators Padilla and Schiff will hear it.

  • Democrats won at least 55 Senate seats under his leadership this month, and he remains the favorite to win the vote. (NBC News)
  • After El-Sayed won his primary, the super PAC aligned with Schumer spent $30 million to help elect him. (JTA)
  • Schumer has criticized Benjamin Netanyahu’s conduct of the war in Gaza while continuing to support military aid. (CNN)
  • Van Hollen is not an outsider. He is 67, has served in Congress for 23 years, and has held party leadership posts himself. (CNN)

We think the record above outweighs these points, and that a new majority elected on a demand for change should have a leader who has not already lost the confidence of its voters.
