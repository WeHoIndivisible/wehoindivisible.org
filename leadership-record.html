Senate leadership vote scheduled for December 1

Schumer and Van Hollen: the records, side by side

Senate Democrats will choose between Chuck Schumer of New York, their leader since 2017, and Chris Van Hollen of Maryland. The vote is scheduled for December 1 and may be moved earlier. Our chapter is asking Senators Padilla and Schiff to vote for Van Hollen. This page lays out the record behind that request, with a source for each item.

The short version

  • When it counted, Schumer reversed himself. In March 2025 he said Republicans lacked the votes to advance their spending bill, then announced the next day that he would vote to advance it. Van Hollen voted no.
  • Schumer has voted against most of his own caucus on arms sales to Israel. In April 2026 he was one of seven Democrats to do so. Van Hollen has voted to block these sales since the first Senate votes.
  • Schumer accepts AIPAC money and Van Hollen does not. Both have raised large sums from the financial sector, Schumer over a much longer period.
  • Schumer’s preferred candidates lost key primaries. He privately backed Haley Stevens over Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan and reportedly preferred Angie Craig over Peggy Flanagan in Minnesota. El-Sayed and Flanagan are now senators-elect.
  • Schumer polled poorly before the election. In an NBC News poll taken in late October, 13 percent of registered voters viewed him positively.

Key votes

March 2025: advancing the Republican spending bill

SchumerVoted yesSaid on March 12 that Republicans did not have the votes to advance the bill, then announced the next day that he would vote to advance it. Nine members of his caucus voted with him, and the motion cleared the 60-vote threshold, 62 to 38.
Van HollenVoted noVoted against advancing the bill, with most of the caucus. Both men voted against final passage, which needed only a simple majority.
Sources: Roll Call, The Fiscal Times

2025 and 2026: blocking arms sales to Israel

SchumerVoted no each timeOpposed both resolutions in July 2025, when more than half of Senate Democrats supported the one on rifles. In April 2026 he was one of seven Democrats to oppose resolutions on bulldozers and bombs.
Van HollenVoted yes each timeSupported the July 2025 and April 2026 resolutions, co-sponsored the 2026 measure on bombs, and has backed these measures since the first Senate votes in November 2024.
Sources: Associated Press, The Nation, The Jewish Chronicle, Sen. Sanders’ office

2015: the Iran nuclear agreement

SchumerOpposed itBroke with President Obama and was the first Senate Democrat to announce opposition to the agreement.
Van HollenSupported itBacked the agreement as a House member and defended it during his 2016 Senate campaign.
Sources: PBS NewsHour, Baltimore Sun

Now: the December funding bill and the war in Iran

SchumerHas not committedVoted for a war powers resolution to end the war, but had not said as of November 13 whether he would block the December funding bill over it.
Van HollenWould withhold fundingSays Democrats should refuse to pass a funding bill that does not end the war, even if that leads to a shutdown.
Sources: The Intercept, Punchbowl News, NBC News

Who funds them

These sources use different definitions and cover different periods, so the figures below should not be added together.

AIPAC and other pro-Israel PACs

SchumerAccepts AIPAC moneyFEC filings show about $95,000 from AIPAC’s PAC, including donations it forwarded from its donors. His total from all tracked pro-Israel PACs, including that amount, is about $516,000.
Van HollenNo AIPAC moneyFEC filings show nothing from AIPAC’s PAC or its super PAC, and he has said no one should accept AIPAC money. He has received about $134,000 from other pro-Israel PACs, chiefly J Street, which has opposed AIPAC on issues such as the Iran agreement.
Sources: Who Funds My Rep: Schumer, Van Hollen

The financial sector

SchumerA top recipient for decadesReceived nearly $9 million from the securities and investment industry from 1989 to 2010, and it was his top contributing industry from 2013 to 2018. Americans for Financial Reform counted $5.35 million from the financial sector for his election cycle ending in 2016.
Van HollenAlso a large recipientAmericans for Financial Reform counted $2.15 million from the financial sector in 2015 and 2016, when he ran for the Senate. That ranked fifth among House members.
Sources: CBS News, Albany Times Union, Americans for Financial Reform (full report)

Corporate PACs

SchumerAccepts themFEC records show contributions from corporate and trade-association PACs, including financial firms.
Van HollenSays he refuses themAccepted corporate PAC money as a House member and later said his campaign rejects it. FEC records show a $1,500 contribution from Bank of America’s PAC in 2022. We could not confirm whether the campaign kept it.
Sources: ProPublica: Schumer, ProPublica: Van Hollen (House), ProPublica: Van Hollen (Senate), Washington Free Beacon

Schumer’s record as leader

  • The March 2025 reversal. House Democrats had voted almost unanimously against the Republican spending bill. Schumer’s change of position divided the party and prompted calls for him to step aside. (Roll Call, The Fiscal Times)
  • Primaries. In Michigan, Schumer privately backed Rep. Haley Stevens over Abdul El-Sayed, then supported El-Sayed in the general election. In Minnesota, Semafor reported that he preferred Rep. Angie Craig over Peggy Flanagan, though PolitiFact found that he endorsed neither. El-Sayed and Flanagan won their primaries and their general elections. AIPAC’s super PAC spent about $20 million opposing El-Sayed and $10.5 million supporting Stevens in the primary, the most it has spent on a single race. (Time, Semafor, PolitiFact, Newsweek)
  • Polling. An NBC News poll in late October found that 13 percent of registered voters viewed Schumer positively. A separate Puck and Echelon Insights poll in November 2025 found that 48 percent of Democratic voters viewed him favorably. The two polls measured different groups. (The Hill, Puck)
  • The new majority. At least six incoming Democratic senators have said they will not vote for him, and Senators Sanders and Slotkin have said the same. Opposing Schumer is not the same as endorsing Van Hollen, and not all of them have. (The Hill)

Van Hollen’s record

  • Experience running a caucus campaign. He chaired the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee from 2017 to 2019 and led House Democrats’ campaign committee before that. (Wikipedia, Yahoo News)
  • What he says he would change. The leader would stay out of open-seat primaries, and the party’s Senate campaign committee would stop accepting AIPAC money. He names restoring Medicaid funding, extending Affordable Care Act tax credits, and restoring nutrition programs as first priorities. (Time, Fox News)

What Van Hollen did for Kilmar Abrego Garcia

In March 2025 the Trump administration deported Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Maryland resident, to a prison in El Salvador despite a 2019 court order that barred his removal to that country. The administration acknowledged the deportation was a mistake, and El Salvador’s president said he would not return him.

  • He went in person. Van Hollen flew to El Salvador on April 16, 2025, after promising the family he would do everything he could.
  • He pressed the government for answers. Van Hollen said Vice President Félix Ulloa told him that El Salvador was holding Abrego Garcia because the Trump administration was paying it to, and that the government offered no evidence of the gang ties the administration had alleged.
  • He kept going after being refused. On April 16 he was denied both a visit and a phone call. On April 17 soldiers stopped his car near the prison, and later that day officials brought Abrego Garcia to his hotel.
  • He made contact. Van Hollen said he was the first person from outside to reach Abrego Garcia since the deportation. He learned that Abrego Garcia had been moved out of the CECOT prison and reported on his condition to his family.
  • He framed it as a constitutional question. He said the trip was about the right to due process, which protects everyone, and not about defending one man.

Sources: NPR, ABC News, 7News.
